01 Our mission

Serious clinical care, at human scale.

A Congregate Living Health Facility (CLHF) is a licensed residential healthcare facility for patients whose needs are too complex for ordinary residential care, but who generally no longer require an acute-care hospital. Care is more medically intensive than a typical skilled nursing setting — 24-hour skilled nursing, ventilator support and management, tracheostomy care, complex wounds, IV therapy — delivered in a six-bed residence where every patient is known by name, by history, and by what matters to them.

A CLHF is not assisted living, a board-and-care home, a nursing home, a hospice residence, or a private home with caregivers. It is a regulated healthcare facility where medically complex residents receive continuing nursing and clinical support in a residential setting.

Our mission: to provide compassionate, clinically responsible, and individualized care for medically complex individuals in a safe, dignified, and home-like residential environment — combining the clinical discipline of a licensed healthcare facility with the personal attention a six-bed residence makes possible.

04 Clinical programs

Planned clinical capabilities

Respiratory & ventilator care

Ventilator support and management, tracheostomy care and suctioning, airway management, oxygen therapy, and respiratory monitoring — in coordination with respiratory therapists, pulmonologists, and equipment providers.

Complex skilled nursing

24-hour skilled nursing: assessments and monitoring, medication administration and management, complex wound care, catheter and ostomy care — with IV therapy and line care based on physician orders and facility capability.

Rehabilitation therapies

Physical, occupational, and speech therapy — provided or coordinated according to each patient's individualized plan of care. Progress is measured in what matters: a first step, a first word, a meal at the table.

Whole-person support

Enteral nutrition and dietary support, social services, recreational programming, family education and care conferences — and discharge and transition planning from the day of admission.

07 Admissions

How a referral becomes an admission

Every referral follows the same structured path — so hospitals, families, and health plans always know where things stand.

  1. 01

    Referral or inquiry

    A hospital, case manager, physician, health plan, family member, or authorized representative contacts Calmora.

  2. 02

    Clinical review

    Records are submitted securely. A qualified clinical professional reviews medical condition, nursing and respiratory needs, medications, nutrition, equipment, rehabilitation needs, and safety risks.

  3. 03

    Capability assessment

    We confirm whether the residence has the license, bed availability, staffing, competencies, and equipment to care for the patient safely.

  4. 04

    Payer verification

    Eligibility, authorization, benefits, and payer requirements are reviewed.

  5. 05

    Transition coordination

    On acceptance, we coordinate physician orders, pharmacy, respiratory and durable medical equipment, nutrition, transport, and the discharge plan.

  6. 06

    Admission & plan of care

    The nursing and interdisciplinary team completes required assessments and begins the individualized plan of care.
